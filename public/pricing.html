WHAT THE ENGAGEMENT COVERS

A clear scope.
A measurable rollout.

Core platform scope. Invoice visibility, routing decisions, approval policy, agent supervision, and reconciliation workflows. Identity controls, retention, and audit exports are specified during solution design.

Volume pricing. Indicative platform usage fees above the monthly allowance: Business 5 basis points, Enterprise 3 basis points, Strategic 1 basis point. One basis point is 0.01%; $1M above an allowance would add $500, $300, or $100 respectively.

Third-party costs. Bank fees, FX spreads, network costs, custody, liquidity, provider charges, and premium data are separate. Included volume is a platform allowance, not a promise of payment availability or zero transaction fees.

Implementation and acceptance. Entity count, systems, data quality, security requirements, and payment corridors determine the final onboarding scope. Testing and acceptance criteria are agreed before rollout.

Commercial details.

Can a small business start with a single automation?

Yes. Invoice capture, approval routing, collections, and reconciliation can be scoped as separate services without purchasing an enterprise platform engagement. Explore services ↗

Is the terminal a live payment service?

The public terminal uses synthetic invoices and simulated actions. Production delivery requires contracted integrations, eligible payment accounts, security validation, and operational readiness. Scope and availability are agreed in writing.

What determines the final subscription?

Payment volume, entities, users, API consumption, integration complexity, support coverage, and control requirements. These are Numina's indicative prices, not claimed market averages or competitor quotes.

How are security and support commitments defined?

Identity integration, permission boundaries, key custody, recovery objectives, retention, incident response, and support hours are agreed in the statement of work. No third-party security certification is claimed by this website.
