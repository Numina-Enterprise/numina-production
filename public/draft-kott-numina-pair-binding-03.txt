Abstract

   This document describes a proposed companion format for associating
   two frozen software-artifact commitments with attribution and
   revisioned claims.  It specifies canonical signing bytes, separate
   trust and freshness checks, claim-making and verification procedures,
   and operational state boundaries.  The intended implementing
   community is software-provenance maintainers, release operators and
   independent security reviewers.  Existing IETF canonicalization and
   signature specifications are used without defining a new
   cryptographic primitive.  Retained source comparisons, document-
   signature checks and synthetic tests are indexed with their limits.
   Application control-plane evidence is distinguished from deployment
   of the companion itself.  A complete operational profile,
   independently authenticated trust and current-state evidence, and a
   verified field deployment remain prerequisites to an implementation-
   readiness claim.

1.  Community and problem statement

   Independent verifiers need a reproducible way to check that a signed
   record binds two specified artifacts to an authorized source and
   revision, without confusing attribution with proof of the record's
   claims.

   The author's intended beneficiaries are all humanity.  The initial
   technical community addressed here is narrower: maintainers of
   software-artifact archives, release operators that preserve existing
   commitments, and independent reviewers deciding whether a provenance
   or attribution statement is supported.  The immediate use case is
   adding or correcting an association without changing the previously
   committed artifacts.  No universal adoption or demonstrated universal
   benefit is claimed.

   This document concerns evidence preservation and bounded acceptance
   of signed statements.  It does not authorize an external action,
   determine disputed human authorship, derive an unknown genesis value,
   or establish a medical, financial or physical-world claim.  Broad
   proposed applications are summarized separately in Section 12.

   The motivating image is plain.  Otters hold hands so they don't
   drift apart while resting: every agent stays connected without
   losing its independence.  Singularity.

   Singularity, here, names the object this document specifies: two
   frozen commitments held in a single signed association -- one
   object for verification, two sources in fact, each keeping its
   own attribution and neither absorbed into the other.  The binding
   is the held hand.  It exists so the pair does not drift apart,
   and it changes neither artifact it joins.

2.  Approach and relationship to IETF work

   Preserve the original artifacts and their hashing procedures.  Create
   a separate companion payload that names their commitments, an
   attribution mapping, a binding identity and revision, and an opaque
   private reference.  Sign the exact canonical payload under a fixed
   application prefix.  A verifier then reports independent findings for
   the bytes, signer authority, source selection, freshness and private
   linkage.  One passing finding never substitutes for another.

   JSON Canonicalization Scheme (JCS), [1], defines canonical bytes for
   the new payload; I-JSON, [3], constrains interoperable JSON; and pure
   Ed25519, [2], supplies the signature primitive.  These do not replace
   the historical source-artifact normalizer.  The model retains its
   numina.pair-binding.v1 identity and original commitment order.

   The RATS architecture, [37], separates evidence appraisal from the
   relying party's policy decision.  That separation is a useful
   architectural comparison: this companion carries an association claim
   and the verifier evaluates evidence under an independently
   provisioned policy.  It is not a hardware-attestation profile or a
   claim of RATS wire interoperability.  COSE, [38], provides
   standardized signed-object structures; this design instead retains
   its existing fixed-prefix JSON construction and is not a COSE object.
   A future COSE profile would require a new explicit wire specification
   and test vectors, not relabeling existing signatures.

   in-toto [7] and DSSE [8] are further comparison points for
   development evidence and signed envelopes.  No novelty or comparative
   security advantage is established.  The additional navigation and
   vision literature is retained as non-normative research context in
   Appendix K; it is not the protocol's IETF rationale.

2.1.  State and memory architecture

   The proposed companion requires distinct stores or equivalently
   isolated records: immutable original artifacts and pinned hash
   profiles; immutable companion revisions containing canonical payload
   and envelope bytes; independently administered key and scope policy;
   an authenticated accepted-revision view; an access-controlled
   private-reference map; and verifier decision records identifying
   evidence, policy version and observation time.  The architecture is a
   requirement for a future companion deployment, not evidence that all
   six components are already operational.

   The accepted-revision view identifies binding_id, revision and
   canonical payload digest.  A verifier checks it against the retained
   predecessor chain and rejects conflicting same-revision digests.
   Caches retain the policy/version/observation context and report age;
   loss of current authority leaves freshness unverified.  Restoration
   must preserve revision monotonicity and prior decisions.  A hash-
   linked log alone does not stop a privileged administrator from
   replacing the whole log and recomputing its hashes.

   The retained Numina application has a different, inspected control-
   state implementation: a durable singleton row, hash-linked events,
   revision-bound actions and caller-scoped projections.  The source
   names its table numina_control_manifest; an older architecture



Kott                      Expires 21 May 2027                 [Page 5]

Internet-Draft                 Singularity                 November 2026


   document says numina_control_state.  This discrepancy is recorded
   rather than hidden.  Application journal revisions, manuscript
   revisions and companion binding revisions are separate counters.  Its
   canonicalization and authority policy must not be assumed equivalent
   to JCS or companion trust. [39] [40]

   Human or agent context is a derived narrative projection, not signing
   authority or canonical accepted state.  A proposed persistent-context
   record carries its source/version, observation time, access class and
   correction history.  Updates invalidate affected cached summaries;
   unavailable provenance remains explicit.  Retention, authorized
   deletion and recovery policy must state which evidence remains
   verifiable after removal.  This describes a design boundary, not
   immutable model memory or automatic history availability to every
   future assistant.

4.  Making a claim and constructing its signature

   The new companion payload is parsed with duplicate-member detection
   and validated against a closed, versioned schema before use.
   Unsupported schemas, unknown fields, missing required fields,
   malformed encodings, and an unexpected number of sources are
   rejected.  Revisions are positive integers within the interoperable
   safe-integer range.  Field-level constraints must be explicit;
   accepting an arbitrary string as an artifact or profile identifier
   would leave the hash's meaning unresolved.

   JCS, specified in RFC 8785, supplies a deterministic UTF-8
   representation of a JSON value [1].  Object properties are ordered
   recursively by UTF-16 code units; array order is retained.
   Whitespace between tokens is omitted.  Strings are preserved without
   Unicode normalization, and invalid Unicode input is rejected.  Number
   serialization follows the specified ECMAScript behavior.  I-JSON
   supplies related interoperability constraints, including unique
   member names [3].  This design additionally limits revisions to safe
   integers and treats hashes and identifiers as strings.




Kott                      Expires 21 May 2027                 [Page 8]

Internet-Draft                 Singularity                 November 2026


   Let payload be the validated payload and C = UTF8(JCS(payload)).  C
   has no byte-order mark or appended newline.  The signature field is
   outside payload.  The following specifies signing; it does not report
   an executed signing operation.  The separator is one zero byte
   (0x00).

   M = ASCII("NUMINA-PAIR-BINDING/V1") || 0x00 || UTF8(JCS(payload))
   signature = Ed25519.Sign(private_key, M)

   Here, || means byte concatenation.  This produces a signature over
   the payload, which contains the source hashes and genesis reference.
   It does not calculate the genesis hash.  No verified genesis-
   derivation equation is available in the evidence we inspected.

   The algorithm is pure Ed25519 as specified in RFC 8032 [2].  No extra
   application prehash is inserted.  In particular, signing SHA-256(M)
   would define a different construction.  The prefix is part of the
   message passed to pure Ed25519; it is not an Ed25519ctx parameter.
   The 64-byte signature and 32-byte public key are represented as
   unpadded base64url.  Decoding must reject malformed input and enforce
   exact lengths.

   The SHA-256 of C identifies the payload for revision linkage.  An
   optional envelope-file digest identifies the exact serialized
   envelope bytes and can differ when formatting changes.  These digest
   roles must be labeled explicitly.  Neither one replaces either frozen
   source commitment.  The construction specifies bytes to sign but
   supplies no operational key, signature, or signed public payload.

4.1.  Claim-making procedure

   1.  Establish the claim's project, release, immutable source,
       intended audience and approved attribution.  Obtain the
       independent signing policy, accepted binding state and permitted
       disclosure scope.  If any required authority or identity is
       unavailable, stop before issuance and retain an explicitly
       incomplete candidate.

   2.  Inventory the original artifacts and original hash-profile code
       by path, version, byte count and raw-file digest.  Reproduce the
       two commitments under those profiles.  Record failures without
       changing the expected hashes or substituting a new normalizer.

   3.  Choose the approved binding_id and next revision under the
       deployment's serialized update policy.  For a successor, copy the
       accepted predecessor's canonical payload digest.  Do not borrow a
       control-journal or manuscript revision.  Resolve the approved
       opaque reference only through the authorized mapping process.



Kott                      Expires 21 May 2027                 [Page 9]

Internet-Draft                 Singularity                 November 2026


   4.  Construct and schema-check the complete payload.  Populate the
       actual issued_at at issuance.  Produce C and M exactly as
       specified above.  Send M to the authorized signer; keep the
       private key out of the payload, report and public package.

   5.  Verify the returned signature against the independently approved
       public key and M before marking issuance complete.  Persist the
       canonical payload, envelope bytes, digests, predecessor and
       issuance receipt without altering the original artifacts.

   6.  Request an atomic update of the authenticated current-state view
       conditioned on the expected predecessor.  Retain conflict and
       failure receipts.  Publish only approved companion bytes after a
       verifier repeats the required checks; record any external deposit
       separately.  No completed issuance or publication is asserted by
       describing this procedure.

5.  Signing authority and trust policy

   A signature can establish consistency between a message and a public
   key.  Acceptance as an authorized Numina statement additionally
   requires an independent trust entry that binds that key to the exact
   project, release, and signing role.  The verifier recomputes the key
   fingerprint from the raw key bytes, matches the approved key material
   and scope, and applies validity and revocation rules defined by the
   trust policy.

   A public key carried inside a payload is an input to verification,
   not an authority to trust itself.  A malicious party can create a
   different key and sign a false attribution statement.  Trust
   bootstrapping must therefore occur through a separately authenticated
   channel.  The trust policy also needs a documented decision about
   evaluating revocation and key validity at issuance, verification
   time, or both; this paper does not assert that such a policy already
   exists.

   An authorized signature attests the signer's association statement.
   Personal identity, authorship, mathematical derivation, and equality
   with current source artifacts each need their own evidence.  The
   absence of any one of those findings must remain visible even when
   the signature check succeeds.

   Ed25519 is also the assistant's user-assigned display name.  That
   label assigns no signing key or signing authority.  In the record
   schema, Ed25519 names the signature algorithm; authorization and key

6.  Verifying claims and rejecting unsupported acceptance

   A verifier produces a structured result rather than one
   undifferentiated success value.  At minimum, it reports schema
   validity, attribution-map validity, payload signature validity,
   signer authorization, source commitment reproduction, current-source
   equality, revision freshness, and private genesis linkage.  Each
   result records the target checked, the evidence used, the observation
   time where relevant, and a reason for any failure or unavailable
   evidence.

6.1.  Verification sequence

   1.  Parse the envelope once under size and depth limits; detect
   duplicate keys before a parser could discard them.  Check the closed
   envelope and payload schemas.  A null signature yields an unsigned
   pending state.  A malformed or failing signature yields an invalid
   state rather than pending.

   2.  Construct C and M exactly as in Section 4.  If signature_b64u is
   null, retain the unsigned pending signature result and skip signature
   decoding and verification.  Otherwise, decode the key and signature,
   enforce lengths and encodings, recompute the key fingerprint, and
   verify with the required Ed25519 variant.  Reuse the same validated
   payload for subsequent decisions.  Unsigned or invalid records cannot
   be accepted as signed; permitted independent diagnostic checks may
   still report separate results.

   3.  Compare project, release, signing role, and immutable source
   identifier with independently expected values.  Evaluate the approved
   trust entry and key policy.  A cryptographically valid statement
   outside its authorized scope is rejected for that scope.



Kott                      Expires 21 May 2027                [Page 11]

Internet-Draft                 Singularity                 November 2026


   4.  Resolve each artifact and its pinned hash profile, recompute the
   original commitments, and compare both outputs.  Separately check
   whether the source being evaluated is the canonical source named in
   scope.  Historical hash reproduction is reported only for the
   snapshot actually inspected.

   5.  Obtain independently trusted current-state evidence for the
   binding identifier and revision.  Check predecessor linkage,
   revocation, and same-revision conflicts.  Resolve genesis_ref only
   through an authorized private channel when that check is available.
   Return the individual results without promoting an unavailable check
   to success.

6.2.  Current state and revision history

   A signed timestamp is the signer's statement about issuance.  It does
   not show that no later revision exists.  A predecessor digest
   establishes a link to an earlier payload; it does not, by itself,
   establish which branch is current.  Acceptance therefore depends on a
   current-state authority or an independently authenticated policy
   source that identifies the accepted revision and payload digest.

   A stale revision, a revoked statement, or divergent payloads at the
   same binding identifier and revision must not be accepted as the
   unique current record.  If current-state evidence is unavailable, the
   correct freshness result is unverified.  A future implementation must
   specify update ordering, rollback handling, evidence caching, and how
   an offline verifier communicates the age of its last trusted state.

9.  Operational considerations

   Keep source artifacts, companion revisions and operational logs under
   distinct identities.  Apply least-privilege issuance, trust-
   administration and resolver roles.  A client or model proposes a
   command; server-side policy determines authorization from current
   state.  Never let a narrative, uploaded record or client-supplied
   status grant itself authority.  The retained application follows this
   separation in its inspected control paths, but a companion service
   needs its own verified integration. [40]

   Specify request, nesting, string and artifact-size limits; time
   budgets; storage quotas; and behavior under exhausted resources
   before deployment.  The application guide reports 16 KiB requests,
   five-second read limits and journal-wide validation, with operation-
   specific bounds; those are application-profile claims, not
   automatically the companion's negotiated limits.  Full-journal replay
   cost grows with retained history.  No measured companion throughput,
   availability, recovery-time objective or restoration drill is
   established. [39]






Kott                      Expires 21 May 2027                [Page 16]

Internet-Draft                 Singularity                 November 2026


   For uncertain writes, reconcile by the persisted operation identity
   and expected revision before retrying.  Preserve conflicting branches
   and failed issuance attempts.  Credential rotation does not erase
   history; recovery must keep binding identity, accepted revisions and
   authenticated policy continuity.  A rollback to an older application
   may remove controls and cannot be called equivalent enforcement.  Do
   not clear nonces, journals or trust records to make a failed check
   pass.

   Record source/build/runtime versions, time synchronization
   assumptions, policy/key changes, observation windows, and operational
   owners.  Keep secret keys and sensitive resolver locations outside
   public reports.  Public-safe evidence must identify what an
   independent reviewer can reproduce, what requires authorized access,
   and what was not available.  An incident runbook is an untested
   procedure until an actual drill or event provides receipts. [41]

   Key lifecycle policy records custodian, purpose, permitted scope,
   policy version and validity/revocation intervals.  Rotation requires
   independent authorization of the new key and an explicit transition
   rule for old signatures; a compromised old key cannot authorize its
   own recovery by signature alone.  Historical records retain their
   original keys and verdict context.  Framing or semantic changes
   require an explicit new schema/profile version and migration tests;
   never reinterpret already signed bytes.

   Monitor rejected signatures, unknown keys, source mismatches, same-
   revision conflicts, unavailable or old current-state evidence,
   resolver denials, quota exhaustion and journal growth.  Define
   responsible operators and escalation thresholds before field
   operation.  No such monitoring deployment or response service is
   demonstrated here.  Record provider request/outcome identities
   separately; idempotent internal commits do not establish an external
   provider effect.

11.  Security Considerations

   This analysis follows the RFC 3552/BCP 72 approach of identifying
   assets, adversaries, attacks, countermeasures and residual risk. [36]
   Protected assets include original evidence bytes, association
   integrity, signing authority, accepted revision state, private
   mappings, audit history and service availability.  An attacker can
   read public artifacts, submit malformed records, replay or reorder
   messages, substitute evidence and keys, or compromise a client.  On-
   path modification and passive observation are considered.  Signing
   hosts, trust administrators, private resolvers and database
   administrators are privileged boundaries; their compromise is not
   solved by a bare signature.

   The table identifies controls required by the companion design, with
   application-level evidence identified separately.  A stated
   mitigation is not an executed test.  The retained record does not
   establish that every mitigation is implemented, independently
   assessed, or field-tested.  Cryptography authenticates specified
   bytes under a key; it does not establish the truth of an authorized
   signer's statement.

   *Threats introduced or exposed by the design*

   T1 Parser and canonicalization ambiguity
      Mechanism: Duplicate members, invalid Unicode, number handling or
      source-order changes can make implementations sign or evaluate
      different values.

      Handling: Reject before decision; parse once, enforce closed
      schema and I-JSON constraints, preserve array order, use JCS for
      the payload only.  Pin the original artifact normalizers.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: Independent cross-implementation
      vectors and approved operational profile and full conformance
      remain outstanding.

   T2 Artifact or profile substitution



Kott                      Expires 21 May 2027                [Page 18]

Internet-Draft                 Singularity                 November 2026


      Mechanism: An attacker substitutes a path, hash profile, registry
      or current source while retaining a familiar attribution label.

      Handling: Compare independently expected scope, raw-file/profile
      pins and registry version; reproduce each original commitment;
      report current-source equality separately.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: A malicious approved artifact
      can still satisfy its hash.  Historical equality does not attest a
      current runtime.

   T3 Key substitution or unauthorized signing
      Mechanism: A self-supplied key signs false attribution or a valid
      signer acts beyond scope.

      Handling: Provision trust independently; recompute fingerprint;
      evaluate project/release/source/role, validity and revocation.  Do
      not promote historical test keys.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: Compromised signer or dishonest
      authorized claims can remain correctly signed; external review and
      recovery are required.

   T4 Replay, rollback or equivocation
      Mechanism: A valid old record or competing same-revision payload
      is represented as uniquely current.

      Handling: Authenticate accepted binding state; check predecessor
      and policy; reject stale/conflicting accepted states and disclose
      cache age.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: A dishonest or unavailable
      current-state authority can conceal updates.  No transparency or
      independent checkpoint service is demonstrated.

   T5 Durable-state or journal compromise
      Mechanism: Deleting/replacing state or rolling back backups
      changes what history appears current.

      Handling: Retain append-only evidence, verify sequence/links/state
      binding, use transactional revision checks and independently
      protected recovery evidence.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: An administrator can rewrite a
      self-contained hash chain.  Independent backup restoration and
      off-system anchoring are unverified.

   T6 Private-reference disclosure or misuse



Kott                      Expires 21 May 2027                [Page 19]

Internet-Draft                 Singularity                 November 2026


      Mechanism: Stable references, resolver responses or debug records
      reveal protected information or enable unauthorized correlation.

      Handling: Use opaque references, authenticated caller-scoped
      resolution, minimized public results and protected audit records.
      Do not fetch untrusted locators from claims.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: Opaque stable identifiers still
      permit correlation; access control and encryption need actual
      deployment verification.

   T7 Transport substitution and interception
      Mechanism: A network attacker observes private data or replaces
      policy/resolver/service responses.

      Handling: Use authenticated confidential transport for protected
      online exchanges and verify object signatures/policy identities
      independently.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: Object signatures do not encrypt
      data; transport authentication does not prove record truth.
      Production endpoint settings remain separately testable.

   T8 Forged client authority, CSRF or hostile content
      Mechanism: A browser request, proxy header, document or model
      output tries to broaden permissions or execute a claim.

      Handling: Reauthorize server-side against current state; validate
      identity ingress and Origin for cookie-authenticated writes; treat
      external text as data.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: Source-visible application
      controls do not prove dispatcher-bypass resistance in the deployed
      topology or cover every future companion route.

   T9 Resource exhaustion
      Mechanism: Oversized/deep JSON, expensive artifact hashing,
      repeated signatures or full-journal reads exhaust compute/storage.

      Handling: Apply explicit profile limits and early rejection, rate/
      time budgets and bounded evidence retrieval; retain failure
      reasons.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: Approved operational limits,
      load-test results and recovery targets for a companion deployment
      are not established; the appendix supplies proposed input limits
      for review.




Kott                      Expires 21 May 2027                [Page 20]

Internet-Draft                 Singularity                 November 2026


   T10 Claim/status confusion
      Mechanism: A passed digest, synthetic test, website publication or
      operator attestation is shown as universal verification.

      Handling: Return independent typed findings with source, time,
      version and limitation; require all policy-required evidence for
      the stated decision.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: Human interfaces can still
      overstate results; review presentation and reliance decisions, not
      only cryptographic code.

   T11 Build or evidence mutation
      Mechanism: Build steps rewrite source, receipts or policy after a
      prior audit/signature.

      Handling: Inventory evidence before/after, pin exact source/build
      artifacts and preserve historical bytes; regenerate only
      maintained projections.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: A current engineering audit is
      still active.  No SLSA level, immutable builder or independently
      secured provenance is established by this draft.

   T12 Unsafe recovery or external effects
      Mechanism: Retries duplicate uncertain operations, or a signed
      statement is treated as permission for unrelated actions.

      Handling: Persist operation identity and outcome, compare
      revisions, reconcile before retry, and keep execution
      authorization separate.

      Residual risk and evidence limit: The inspected control plane
      records internal effects; independent external execution or
      settlement requires its own authority and receiving-system
      evidence.

12.  Proposed applications and research boundaries

   The author proposes applications in chemistry, physics, healthcare,
   humanitarian work and civil-rights records.  The concrete capability
   described here is preserving evidence and traceable claim revisions
   for authorized workflows.  Historical records can receive append-only
   corrections without erasing the earlier record; the protocol does not
   alter past physical events.  Present actions require separate
   authorization.  Future-event or scientific predictions require
   predefined targets, independent observations, held-out evaluation,
   baselines and uncertainty reporting.  No medical efficacy, physical
   forecasting, or universal next-action capability is demonstrated by
   this record format.

   The intended humanitarian value is improved accountability for
   evidence and authorized decisions.  Ed25519 is the existing signature
   algorithm defined in RFC 8032, not a newly invented algorithm.
   Neither contribution rights, an IETF submission, an institutional
   inquiry nor a signed record gives this system control over unrelated
   networks or people.  Economic value, scientific recognition and
   regulatory status are separate questions that require their own
   evidence; no guaranteed outcome is asserted.

Appendix Q.  Kotts Law

   Kotts Law is a stated principle of the Numina work, stated by
   Samuel J. Kott on 19 November 2026.  It is recorded here in the
   author's words.

   "Every distinct object carries one singular identity, advancing
   in equal linear steps along a horizontal progression -- rising
   forever, never falling, never reversing.  An eternal upward
   trajectory."

   The law has four clauses:

   1.  Distinct objects.  Every object in the progression is
       distinct from every other.
   2.  Singular identities.  Each object carries one singular
       identity, and only one.
   3.  Horizontal and linear progression.  The progression moves
       horizontally, in equal linear steps.
   4.  Eternal upward trajectory.  The trajectory climbs upward
       forever -- never decreasing, never reversing, no ceiling.

   What it is not.  Kotts Law is not a claim about Newtonian
   mechanics.  Newton's three laws of motion stand as they are.
   Kotts Law is a stated principle of the Numina work, labelled as
   such everywhere it appears.  In the author's words, Murphy's law
   has run its course, and Newton's laws keep things in motion;
   Kotts Law is the progression that motion follows -- upward, for
   eternity.

   Immutability.  Kotts Law does not change, and neither does any
   state it has already produced.  The statement is frozen.  Every
   step returns a new frozen progression; the previous progression
   is never rewritten, never mutated, never re-pointed.  History is
   preserved exactly as it happened, for eternity.

   Implementation.  The law is implemented and verified as code in
   the Numina codebase (src/numina-kotts-law.js), with twelve tests
   passing (tests/numina-kotts-law.test.mjs).  The implementation
   refuses a duplicate or empty identity and refuses a
   non-positive step; each step moves every object exactly one
   horizontal step forward and exactly one climb upward; the
   recorded history is verified singular, exactly linear, and
   strictly increasing at every step, never reversing.
