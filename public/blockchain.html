01 / THE FOUNDATION

A shared record.
Rules that can execute.

A blockchain is a ledger maintained by a network of computers. Participants submit signed transactions; the network applies its rules to agree on a consistent history. Cryptographic links make changing accepted records detectable, while the network's consensus process determines which transactions become part of the ledger.

A wallet manages the keys used to authorize an action. A token is a record of a digital asset or claim. A smart contract is a program executed by the network—for example, a rule governing who can transfer an asset or when a payment can be released. On Ethereum, these programs share a common execution environment. Ethereum technical introduction ↗

AuthorizeSign an instruction
ValidateCheck rules & balances
ConfirmReach network agreement
ReconcileMatch the business record

Finality is the point at which an accepted transaction is considered irreversible under a network's rules. It is different from a fast user-interface response. An instruction can be submitted quickly while confirmations, compliance checks, and conversion into bank money take longer.

02 / THE APPLICATION

What changes
for a business payment?

Today, a cross-border invoice can involve separate systems for payment instructions, currency conversion, bank settlement, and reconciliation. A blockchain-based workflow can link the transfer of a digital asset to a shared transaction record and programmable business rules.

For business payments, a stablecoin is often more practical than a volatile asset. Stablecoins aim to track a reference value such as one U.S. dollar; the design may depend on issuer reserves, collateral, or other mechanisms. A token targeting one dollar is not the same as an insured bank deposit, and redemption conditions and depegging risk still matter. How stablecoins work ↗

Always-available rails

Public networks can accept transactions outside banking hours. The complete payout still depends on the availability of funding, conversion, and local banking partners.

Programmable conditions

A workflow can tie release to approved counterparties or a completed business condition. Off-chain facts need trustworthy data and appropriate dispute handling.

A common audit trail

Transaction identifiers help reconcile the movement of value. Accounting, identity, invoices, and tax records still need to connect to that trail.

Machine-to-machine commerce

Standards such as x402 explore per-use stablecoin payments for resources like API calls, including agent-driven use. Ethereum payment examples ↗

Consider a supplier invoice: the buyer verifies the beneficiary, funds a permitted stablecoin balance, approves a quote, submits the transfer, waits for the required confirmation, and records the recipient's payout. This is an illustrative workflow—not a claim that every currency corridor is available or cheaper.

FROM INVOICE TO CASH APPLICATION

A payment is a business process.

The transaction on a blockchain is one step. A finance team also needs to know what is owed, whether the supplier is legitimate, which entity is paying, who approved it, and when the beneficiary can actually use the money. Numina's focus is on coordinating that complete process.

01 / Capture and match

Extract invoice fields, check for duplicates, and compare the invoice against a purchase order and goods receipt. Partial deliveries, tax differences, and changed bank details enter an exception queue instead of triggering a payment.

02 / Decide when to pay

Compare due dates, cash forecasts, and early-payment discounts. Keep minimum operating balances and expected payroll separate from funds available for supplier settlement. A cheaper route is not useful if it misses a delivery deadline.

03 / Compare the full route

Evaluate bank transfer, eligible tokenized deposits, or a permitted stablecoin route. Add funding charges, FX spreads, network costs, payout fees, and liquidity constraints. Check recipient currency, cutoff times, and quote expiry before requesting approval.

04 / Release and reconcile

Bind approvals to the exact beneficiary, amount, currency, and route. Track provider acceptance separately from network finality and bank payout. Match the receipt to the invoice and general ledger; handle failed or partial payouts without submitting duplicates.

A supplier example

A U.S. distributor owes an overseas manufacturer $84,250 after receipt of a shipment. Its accounts-payable agent matches the invoice to the purchase order and warehouse receipt. Treasury compares a wire with a supported digital route, checks the manufacturer's payout requirements, and approves the instruction. A reconciliation agent then links the provider reference, payment receipt, and invoice balance.

If the warehouse receipt is missing or supplier banking details changed, payment stays on hold. The example illustrates an operating design, not a live Numina customer transaction.

Three forms of digital value

Stablecoins

Issuer-backed tokens can move over public networks. Treasury must evaluate reserve quality, redemption eligibility, concentration limits, and payout liquidity. Holding a token is not the same as holding a bank deposit.

Tokenized bank deposits

These represent commercial-bank deposits within an eligible banking arrangement. Access can be limited to participating banks, locations, currencies, and onboarded clients. HSBC, for example, describes transfers across its private network. HSBC service details ↗

Tokenized investments

A fund token represents an investment interest, not cash. Subscription, redemption, transfer restrictions, and market risk still apply. Tokenized money-market funds may support treasury workflows, but do not automatically pay a supplier invoice.

Traditional rails remain essential

Domestic instant payments, wires, and local payout partners may be the better route. A useful orchestration layer compares eligibility and total economics instead of assuming every payment belongs on a blockchain.

CORPORATE TREASURY / REPORTED CLIENT USE

J.P. Morgan: programmable treasury and FX

J.P. Morgan reported new Kinexys mandates from Siemens and B2C2 in October 2025. Siemens expanded its treasury use to on-chain FX. This is a direct corporate-money use case: managing liquidity across currencies and operating hours. It is distinct from buying cryptocurrency for investment.

CASH MANAGEMENT / SERVICE AND INTEGRATION

Citi: tokenized cash connected to bank clearing

Citi's September 2025 announcement described connecting Token Services with its 24/7 USD Clearing network for institutional clients. Its private blockchain supports internal liquidity transfers; the clearing connection extends the payment workflow toward supplier and third-party accounts. The value is connectivity between systems, subject to account and market eligibility.

Citi announcement ↗
CHAINLINK + SWIFT + UBSCOMPLETED PILOT · NOV 2024

Connecting tokenized funds to existing payment systems.

Swift, UBS Asset Management, and Chainlink completed a pilot that used existing Swift infrastructure for the cash settlement of tokenized fund subscriptions and redemptions. The important distinction: the cash leg did not require every institution to adopt an on-chain currency.

Chainlink coordinated interactions between the systems. This illustrates interoperability between a tokenized asset workflow and established payment infrastructure, rather than replacing all banking rails at once.

Read the Swift announcement ↗
ETHEREUM + CHAINLINK + UBSLIVE TRANSACTION · NOV 2025

A tokenized fund workflow in production.

UBS reported a live subscription and redemption workflow for uMINT, its USD money market investment fund built on Ethereum. DigiFT acted as the on-chain distributor, using Chainlink's Digital Transfer Agent standard.

The use case joins order processing, settlement, and synchronization of records. Ethereum supplies programmable infrastructure; Chainlink provides coordination capabilities. For business finance, the architectural lesson is that a shared ledger still needs reliable instructions and synchronized records across the fund, distributor, and payment systems. A reported live transaction is tangible evidence, but is not proof that every fund or market has migrated.

Read the UBS announcement ↗
STELLAR + FRANKLIN TEMPLETONLIVE FUND · SINCE 2021

Recording fund ownership on a public blockchain.

Franklin Templeton launched the Franklin OnChain U.S. Government Money Fund on Stellar in April 2021. The public blockchain serves as the official record for processing transactions and recording share ownership.

Known by its BENJI token, the fund demonstrates tokenization of an actual financial product. The underlying asset remains a regulated fund with its own eligibility, investment, and transfer rules. A token does not remove those obligations. Stellar is a payment-oriented network, while BENJI is a fund use case on that network. For an invoice workflow, treasury would still need eligible redemption and a supported recipient payout route; fund ownership alone does not settle the liability.

Read the Stellar case study ↗
VISA + USDC + SOLANAU.S. LAUNCH · DEC 2025

Stablecoin settlement inside a payment network.

Visa announced U.S. USDC settlement with initial participants Cross River Bank and Lead Bank settling over Solana. The program allows participating institutions to settle obligations in USDC with seven-day availability while keeping the consumer card experience unchanged.

This is a settlement-layer use case: it does not mean all Visa transactions occur on a blockchain. It shows how digital assets can modernize a specific part of an established financial network.

04 / THE PRACTICAL REALITY

The technology is real.
The details still decide.

Blockchain can reduce coordination friction, but business value depends on the full route. Compare the total cost of funding, FX spread, network fees, liquidity, payout, and reconciliation—not just the transaction fee shown by a network.

  • Custody & authorization. Lost keys, compromised approvals, and contract bugs can cause irreversible losses.
  • Liquidity & redemption. A digital balance must be usable where the recipient needs it. Issuer and banking dependencies remain.
  • Compliance & privacy. Identity checks, sanctions screening, jurisdictional rules, and data handling belong in the design.
  • Prediction & execution. A signal is an estimate, not advance knowledge. Fast execution does not guarantee a fill, a profit, or immediate settlement.

Numina's product vision is to connect these layers in a controlled operating environment: observe conditions, evaluate a route or action, obtain the required approval, and preserve the audit trail.
